We further assessed whether differential attrition from Wave~1 ($n = 2927$)
to the balanced panel ($n = 1300$; retention rate = 44.4\%) biases our
estimates. We modelled the probability of retention as a function of Wave-1
demographics, education, employment status, generalized trust, and
subjective well-being (pseudo-$R^2 = 0.018$), and computed stabilized
inverse-probability weights (IPW) as $\hat{w}_i = \bar{p} /
\hat{P}(\text{retained}|\mathbf{X}_i)$, trimmed at the 99th percentile
(weight range: 0.69--1.51; mean = 1.00). Re-estimating the exit-from-$\gamma$
probit with these weights leaves results substantively unchanged:
AME(high education) $= -0.228$ (SE $= 0.066$, $p < .001$) and
AME(employed) $= -0.144$ (SE $= 0.067$, $p = 0.032$), compared to unweighted
estimates of -0.220 and -0.149, respectively (Table~\ref{table:SI_A7}).
Differential attrition does not appear to confound the structural precarity
results.
